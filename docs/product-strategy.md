# Pet Services Platform: Product Strategy and OKRs

**Planning horizon:** 12 months

**Proposed initial market:** United States

**Status:** Strategy proposal; targets and assumptions require validation

## 1. Executive Summary

Zoetis has an opportunity to connect pet owners, veterinary clinics, and marketing teams through a **clinic-centered preventive-care engagement platform**. The platform would combine trusted vaccine education, clinic-approved reminders, appointment pathways, and eligible promotions for vaccines, medicines, and pet food.

The strategic choice is to **complement, not replace, existing clinic systems**. Begin with a limited pilot that tests whether the platform increases verified vaccinations and promotion redemption without adding clinic workload or compromising clinical independence.

**Year-one ambition:** Establish a repeatable, compliant model that improves vaccination completion, earns clinic participation, and demonstrates positive incremental campaign economics. Targets below are planning hypotheses, not forecasts.

**Leadership decisions requested:**

1. Approve a U.S. pilot focused on dogs and cats, with vaccination as the lead use case.
2. Fund discovery, legal and clinical review, limited integration, and a controlled pilot before committing to broad rollout.
3. Assign accountable business, veterinary, technology, privacy, and regulatory owners; validate whether food promotions require external partners.

## 2. Vision Statement

A world where every pet owner can easily access trusted preventive care, and every veterinary clinic can build lasting relationships that support healthier pets.

## 3. Product Mission

Make it easier for pet owners to understand and act on their veterinary team's recommendations by connecting relevant education, reminders, services, and eligible offers across their preferred channels.

## 4. Strategic Themes

| Theme | Strategic intent |
|---|---|
| Prevention first | Increase awareness and completion of veterinarian-recommended vaccination and preventive care. |
| Clinic partnership | Fit existing workflows, preserve clinical independence, and deliver measurable clinic value. |
| Relevant engagement | Deliver timely, consent-based communications across appropriate channels. |
| Responsible commercial growth | Connect approved promotions to genuine customer needs and measure incremental outcomes. |

## 5. Business Objectives and OKRs

**Measurement approach:** Establish baselines in months 1-3. The following year-one targets are proposed pilot targets, subject to feasibility, legal review, sample-size assessment, and baseline confirmation. Where practicable, compare eligible intervention and control cohorts at the clinic or owner level.

| Objective | ID | Proposed key result by month 12 |
|---|---|---|
| O1. Improve preventive-care action | O1-KR1 | Increase the share of eligible pets with a verified vaccination within 90 days of becoming due by **10% relative** versus a comparable control. |
| O1 | O1-KR2 | Improve vaccine-awareness survey scores by **10 percentage points** from baseline among surveyed target owners. |
| O2. Earn clinic participation | O2-KR1 | Onboard **50 clinics**. |
| O2 | O2-KR2 | Achieve **70% monthly active participation** among onboarded clinics. |
| O2 | O2-KR3 | Achieve median clinic satisfaction of **at least 4/5**. |
| O3. Demonstrate responsible promotional value | O3-KR1 | Achieve **15% redemption** among uniquely eligible owners who receive an offer. |
| O3 | O3-KR2 | Demonstrate positive incremental contribution after incentive and campaign costs in at least **one controlled campaign**. |
| O4. Build sustained customer engagement | O4-KR1 | Reach **40% verified-contact opt-in** among invited eligible owners. |
| O4 | O4-KR2 | Achieve **30% quarterly active engagement** among opted-in owners. |
| O4 | O4-KR3 | Keep opt-outs below **3% per campaign**. |
| O5. Establish a trusted operating model | O5-KR1 | Obtain documented clinical, legal, regulatory, and privacy approval for **100% of launched campaigns**. |
| O5 | O5-KR2 | Resolve **95% of identified high-severity issues within agreed service levels**. |

The 90-day vaccination outcome at month 12 applies to due-pet cohorts observed through month 9. Early cohorts should be monitored throughout the pilot; statistical confidence and sample size will determine whether measured lift is conclusive.

## 8. Market Challenges

- Owners may not understand vaccine timing, disease risks, or the importance of follow-up.
- Clinic staff have limited time to manage additional communications or platforms.
- Education, booking, reminders, records, and offers often live in separate systems.
- Price sensitivity may delay preventive care, while indiscriminate discounting can erode value.
- Records, consent, and outcome data may be incomplete or inconsistent across clinics.
- Promotional messaging must not override clinical judgment or make unsupported claims.

## 9. Competitive Landscape

| Category | Examples | Strategic implication |
|---|---|---|
| Veterinary communication and engagement | PetDesk and similar platforms | Compete on convenience and clinic workflow; integrate or partner where duplication would create friction. |
| Practice management and diagnostics ecosystems | IDEXX and Covetrus | Established clinic relationships and workflow integration create strong incumbency. |
| Animal-health manufacturers | Merck Animal Health, Boehringer Ingelheim Animal Health, Elanco | Compete for clinical relationships, product preference, and engagement around preventive care. |
| Retail, pharmacy, and pet-food ecosystems | Chewy and major pet-food brands | Offer strong consumer reach, convenience, and promotions; may also be potential partners. |

**Proposed differentiation:** Combine Zoetis animal-health expertise and approved education with clinic-led workflows, relevant offers, and verified care outcomes. This advantage must be proven against clinics' existing tools, not assumed.

## 10. Customer Problems to Solve

1. **Owners:** "I do not know what care is due, why it matters, or how to act."
2. **Veterinarians:** "Owners miss recommended preventive care, but generic marketing can undermine trust."
3. **Clinic managers:** "Our team cannot manage another labor-intensive engagement system."
4. **Marketing teams:** "We cannot consistently connect campaign exposure to verified outcomes and incremental value."
5. **All parties:** "We need transparent consent, trustworthy information, and appropriate handling of pet and owner data."

## 12. Key Capabilities

**Minimum viable pilot**

- Owner and pet profiles, with verified contact details and communication preferences.
- Clinic enrollment, permissions, and configurable participation settings.
- Clinically reviewed vaccine education and clinic-approved reminder workflows.
- Email and SMS where consent and market rules permit; accessible web landing pages.
- Clinic contact or booking links, using existing systems when available.
- Offer eligibility, unique codes, redemption capture, and clear terms.
- Campaign approval, audit trail, consent management, and opt-out handling.
- Reporting for funnel activity, verified vaccinations, redemptions, and clinic workload.

**Later, subject to pilot evidence**

- Practice-management system integrations for due dates and vaccination outcomes.
- Medicine and food promotions with appropriate partner, eligibility, and clinical controls.
- Experimentation, segmentation, next-best-action support, and expanded partner connectivity.

**Clinical boundary:** The platform supports education and follow-through; it does not diagnose, prescribe, independently determine a vaccination schedule, or replace veterinary advice.

## 13. North Star Metric

### Incremental verified on-time vaccination completion

**Definition:** Percentage-point difference between intervention and comparable control cohorts in the proportion of eligible pets with a clinic-verified vaccination completed within 90 days after its clinic-defined due date.

- **Denominator:** Unique eligible pets with a documented due date in the observation cohort.
- **Numerator:** Those pets with a verified qualifying vaccination within the 90-day window.
- **Reporting:** Show intervention and control rates, sample sizes, confidence intervals, missing-data rates, and results by clinic and relevant owner segment.
- **Guardrails:** Clinic workload, opt-outs, clinical safety, equitable access, and campaign economics.

This metric links engagement to an observed care outcome rather than treating clicks or redemptions as evidence of improved health. O1-KR1 expresses the same comparison as relative lift.

## 14. Product Success Metrics

| Area | Metric and definition |
|---|---|
| Awareness | Percentage of surveyed target owners correctly answering agreed vaccine-awareness questions; use a consistent baseline and survey method. |
| Clinic adoption | Monthly active clinics divided by onboarded clinics; define "active" as completing a meaningful action such as reviewing a campaign or using reporting. |
| Owner activation | Owners with a verified contact method and required marketing consent divided by eligible owners invited. |
| Engagement | Opted-in owners completing a meaningful action during a quarter divided by opted-in owners eligible for engagement that quarter. |
| Care conversion | Verified vaccinations within 90 days divided by eligible pets due, compared with control. |
| Promotion redemption | Unique eligible owners with verified redemption divided by unique eligible owners receiving an offer; report separately by category and campaign. |
| Economics | Incremental contribution from the intervention versus control, less incremental incentives, delivery, and campaign costs; document attribution assumptions. |
| Trust and operations | Opt-out rate, consent complaints, data completeness, clinic time spent, campaign approval compliance, and incident resolution. |

## 15. Product Risks

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Low clinic adoption or added workload | Weak reach and unreliable outcomes | Co-design with clinics; integrate selectively; track staff time and satisfaction; provide a low-tech fallback. |
| Inappropriate clinical messaging | Loss of trust or patient harm | Veterinary review, clinic-approved protocols, and escalation to a clinician; no autonomous clinical advice. |
| Privacy or consent failure | Customer harm, regulatory exposure, reputational damage | Data minimization, purpose-specific permissions, access controls, retention limits, and auditable opt-outs. |
| Misleading claims or ineligible promotions | Legal and reputational exposure | Market-specific legal and regulatory review, approved claims library, eligibility checks, and documented offer terms. |
| Weak attribution or incomplete records | Misleading performance claims | Controlled experiments, data-quality monitoring, transparent missing-data reporting, and conservative interpretation. |
| Over-discounting | Reduced margin without incremental demand | Holdout groups, campaign-level contribution measurement, incentive caps, and stop rules. |
| Integration delays | Delayed launch or limited scale | Validate data access early; pilot with approved manual or file-based workflows where appropriate. |
| Biased personalization | Unequal access or irrelevant messaging | Test outcomes across segments; limit sensitive inputs; provide human oversight and non-digital alternatives. |

## 16. Assumptions

- The U.S. is the initial market; market-specific rules and expansion requirements will be validated.
- Dogs and cats are the initial species; vaccine schedules and eligibility remain clinic-defined.
- Clinics will permit appropriate access to the minimum data needed for reminders and outcome measurement.
- Existing Zoetis capabilities or partners can support the proposed journeys; build-versus-partner decisions remain open.
- Pet food is a **potential partner-supported category**, not an assumed Zoetis-owned product portfolio.
- Medicines and vaccines may require different promotional, dispensing, and regulatory controls. Availability and eligibility vary by product and jurisdiction.
- The pilot can recruit enough clinics and comparable eligible pets to estimate meaningful impact; this must be tested before launch.
- Targets are provisional and will be reset after baselines and pilot economics are known.

## 17. 12-Month Roadmap

| Period | Focus and deliverables | Decision gate |
|---|---|---|
| Months 1-3: Discover and define | Interview owners and clinics; map existing Zoetis capabilities and competitors; establish baselines; select pilot clinics; assess data access, consent, clinical and legal requirements; define experiment and business case. | Approve pilot scope only if clinic demand, compliant data access, and measurement feasibility are demonstrated. |
| Months 4-6: Build and launch pilot | Deliver core education, consent, reminders, booking links, approved offers, and reporting; onboard first clinics; test intervention and control groups. | Continue only if content and privacy approvals are complete, workflows are usable, and data quality meets agreed thresholds. |
| Months 7-9: Optimize and expand | Improve onboarding and messaging; expand toward 50 clinics; introduce prioritized integration; test selected vaccine and non-vaccine offers; review early economics and workload. | Expand only if adoption, safety, and early commercial signals justify investment. |
| Months 10-12: Prove and decide | Assess mature 90-day care cohorts, redemption, clinic experience, and contribution; identify scalable partnerships and AI opportunities; prepare year-two investment case. | Decide whether to scale, revise, or stop based on measured lift, confidence, economics, and guardrails. |

## 18. Innovation Opportunities: AI and Personalization

| Opportunity | Potential value | Safeguard |
|---|---|---|
| Reminder timing and channel optimization | Improve response using consented preferences and observed engagement. | Frequency caps, opt-outs, controlled testing, and no use of sensitive data without a valid basis. |
| Personalized education | Surface approved content relevant to pet species, life stage, and clinic-provided care context. | Use approved content only; no individualized diagnosis or treatment recommendation. |
| Marketing content assistance | Help teams draft variants and identify missing information. | Human medical, legal, and regulatory review before publication; retain version history. |
| Clinic insights | Highlight aggregated engagement gaps and operational trends. | Minimum cohort sizes, role-based access, and transparent data definitions. |
| Offer relevance | Reduce irrelevant promotions and incentive waste. | Apply eligibility rules first; test fairness and incremental value; never prioritize offers over clinical advice. |

**Sequence:** Start with rules-based segmentation and approved content. Introduce AI only after consent, data quality, approval workflows, and measurement are reliable.

## 19. Dependencies

- **Clinical and regulatory:** Veterinary leadership, medical review, legal, pharmacovigilance or relevant safety processes, and market-specific promotional review.
- **Clinic operations:** Recruitment, agreements, onboarding, training, workflow ownership, and support.
- **Technology and data:** Identity, consent, messaging, analytics, redemption verification, security, and feasible practice-management integrations.
- **Commercial:** Campaign budgets, product eligibility, incentive funding, and partner agreements for food or other third-party offers.
- **Measurement:** Baseline data, control design, clinic-verified outcomes, contribution methodology, and analytics capacity.
- **Governance:** An executive sponsor and cross-functional product owner with authority to make scope, investment, and launch decisions.
