# Product Strategy: Long-Term Medication Support

**Audience:** Executive leadership, veterinary commercial, medical, digital, and clinic engagement teams<br>
**Planning horizon:** 12 months<br>
**Proposed market:** United States, subject to legal and regulatory review<br>
**Working name:** Care Together

## 1. Vision Statement

Make long-term medication care easier for every pet owner, with veterinarians and caregivers connected around the pet's ongoing needs.

## 2. Product Mission

Help pet owners confidently follow their veterinarian's prescribed routine through practical reminders, shared coordination, useful education, and timely prompts to contact their veterinary team. The product supports veterinary care; it does not diagnose, prescribe, adjust medication, or replace a veterinarian.

## 3. Strategic Themes

| Theme | Strategic intent |
|---|---|
| Make daily care simpler | Reduce the effort of remembering, administering, and documenting medication. |
| Strengthen the clinic-owner connection | Prepare useful context for veterinary conversations without creating an unmonitored clinical inbox. |
| Enable coordinated caregiving | Give household members a shared view of the latest recorded medication status. |
| Engage across the journey | Connect clinic, mobile web, email, and other consented channels around owner needs. |
| Build trust by design | Separate care support from promotion and apply privacy, clinical, legal, and safety review. |
| Prove incremental value | Measure owner outcomes, clinic participation, redemption, and business impact against baselines or controls. |

## 4. Business Objectives

These are proposed planning hypotheses, not baselines or forecasts.

| Objective | Proposed year-one ambition |
|---|---|
| Increase awareness | Reach 10,000 eligible owners with a measurable program invitation. |
| Improve clinic participation | Activate 100 clinics, with at least 60 active in the final pilot month. |
| Drive appropriate redemption | Establish a baseline; target a 10% eligible-offer redemption rate in tested campaigns. |
| Improve engagement | Achieve 40% week-four engagement among activated households. |
| Establish evidence for scale | Complete at least one controlled campaign or phased-rollout evaluation. |

Reach and participation depend on clinic volume, permissions, funding, and feasibility. Validate targets after discovery.

## 5. Customer Segments

| Segment | Need | Initial priority |
|---|---|---|
| Owners newly starting long-term medication | Understand instructions and establish a routine. | Primary |
| Owners managing an established chronic regimen | Maintain routines, coordinate care, and plan refills. | Primary |
| Multi-caregiver households | Avoid uncertainty over who administered medication. | Primary |
| Veterinarians and veterinary technicians | Reinforce the care plan and receive useful owner context. | Primary |
| Clinic managers and practice teams | Introduce the program with minimal staff effort. | Primary |
| Zoetis marketing and commercial teams | Deliver reviewed campaigns and measure performance. | Primary |
| Owners managing complex or injectable regimens | Specialized workflows and clinical support. | Later expansion |

The initial product scope is dogs prescribed a daily oral chronic medication in the US. This is a feasibility constraint, not a claim that other animals or therapies have lower need.

## 6. User Personas

| Persona | Motivation | Friction | Product response |
|---|---|---|---|
| Maya, pet owner | Keep her dog comfortable and follow the vet's plan. | Busy mornings, uncertainty, and refill anxiety. | Simple reminders, care notes, refill prompts, and clinic contact. |
| Sam, shared caregiver | Help without making a mistake. | Does not know whether Maya already gave medication. | Shared, timestamped status with caregiver attribution. |
| Dr. Lee, veterinarian | Maintain treatment continuity and understand progress. | Limited time and incomplete owner recall. | Concise owner-shared summary, not a new clinical inbox. |
| Jordan, clinic manager | Support clients without added administration. | Staff training burden and multiple systems. | Simple enrollment materials and limited setup. |
| Alex, Zoetis marketer | Reach the right audience and evaluate campaigns. | Fragmented reporting, consent requirements, and approval delays. | Approved templates, eligibility controls, and aggregate reporting. |

Personas are illustrative and require research validation.

## 7. Market Challenges

1. Chronic care happens outside the clinic, where owners translate instructions into daily behavior.
2. Conditions, treatments, administration difficulty, caregiver availability, and digital access vary.
3. Clinic attention is scarce, and another workflow can add burden.
4. Owners may already use pharmacy, practice, reminder, or messaging tools.
5. A pharmaceutical-company experience must earn clinical and commercial trust.
6. Recorded doses are self-reported, and redemptions are not necessarily incremental.
7. Promotional claims, communications, data use, and adverse-event handling require market-specific review.

## 8. Competitive Landscape

| Category | Examples | Existing strength | Opportunity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Veterinary engagement platforms | [PetDesk](https://petdesk.com/) | Clinic communications, reminders, mobile access, and refill requests. | Complement workflows with medication and caregiver support. |
| Online pet pharmacies | Chewy, PetMeds | Convenient ordering and refills. | Support daily care and clinic conversations regardless of dispensing location. |
| Practice management systems | Clinic-selected software | Embedded clinical and operational workflows. | Start with low-integration introductions; assess integrations when demand is proven. |
| General reminder tools | Phone reminders, calendars, notes | Familiar and low-friction. | Add shared status, care context, refill estimates, and structured summaries. |
| Manual coordination | Paper instructions, pillboxes, household messages | Accessible and inexpensive. | Preserve simplicity while making handoffs and history easier. |

This category-level assessment is not an exhaustive feature comparison. Verify capabilities, pricing, integration access, and market share during discovery.

## 9. Customer Problems to Solve

| Problem | Desired outcome |
|---|---|
| Owners forget doses or refill timing. | Owners know what is due and can plan ahead. |
| Medication can be difficult to administer. | Owners can record difficulties and find reviewed practical resources. |
| Owners do not know what to expect. | Owners have clear general education and easy access to veterinary advice. |
| Caregivers lack a shared record. | A current shared view shows reported actions. |
| Owners struggle to recall changes. | A concise history supports veterinary conversations. |
| Clinics have limited capacity. | Introduction and follow-up fit clinic operations. |
| Marketing has fragmented reporting. | Consented aggregate measurement connects channels and offers. |

Research with 12-15 owners and 5-8 veterinarians or veterinary technicians should test these hypotheses, including actual routines, difficult doses, refill processes, and shared-care handoffs.

## 10. Value Proposition

- **Owners:** One place to manage medication, coordinate care, and prepare for veterinary conversations.
- **Veterinarians:** A practical extension of care instructions that supports useful owner context.
- **Clinic managers:** A lightweight program that does not replace existing systems.
- **Zoetis marketing:** A trusted, measurable channel connecting reviewed education and approved offers to owner needs.

The experience must be useful even to owners who never redeem an offer. Product and treatment decisions remain with the veterinarian and owner.

## 11. Key Capabilities

| Initial capability | Constraint |
|---|---|
| Owner-entered medication instructions, schedule, and clinic contact | Owners check details against the prescription; the app does not set or change the regimen. |
| Reminders and shared dose records | Outcomes are self-reported; missed-dose guidance directs owners to instructions or their veterinary team. |
| Caregiver invitations and status | Prevent conflicting records for the same scheduled dose and flag stale offline status. |
| Refill planning | Remaining supply is an owner-input estimate, not refill authorization. |
| Observations and shareable summaries | Logs are not monitored; owners choose whether and how to share. |
| Education and help | Reviewed resources and clinic/emergency guidance; no diagnosis or dose adjustment. |
| Clinic participation kit | QR code, staff talking points, and owner handout fit existing workflows. |
| Approved offers and aggregate reporting | Separate consent, eligibility, claims review, and clinician independence apply. |

Later capabilities include pharmacy and practice-system integrations, complex schedules, more species and medications, injectables, and clinic-facing workflows only after evidence of demand, staffing, and operational ownership.

## 12. North Star Metric

**Weekly Supported Care Households:** Distinct enrolled households with an active medication routine completing at least one meaningful care-support action in a week: recording a scheduled outcome, adding an observation, updating refill information, or generating a visit summary.

Report the number of eligible enrolled households alongside it. This measures engagement, not verified adherence or improved pet health. Guardrails include owner-reported ease of care, reminder opt-outs, safety incidents, and clinic burden.

## 13. Product Success Metrics

All targets are provisional.

| Area | Definition | Proposed use or target |
|---|---|---|
| Awareness | Owners reached / estimated eligible owners in participating clinic populations | Establish baseline; report numerator and denominator. |
| Clinic activation | Clinics introducing the program to at least one owner / clinics enrolled | 70% within 30 days. |
| Ongoing clinic participation | Clinics introducing the program in a month / clinics enrolled | 60% in the final pilot month. |
| Owner activation | Households completing setup and recording one dose within 7 days / households enrolled | 60%. |
| Week-four engagement | Activated households completing a meaningful action in week four / activated households | 40%. |
| Ease of care | Owners reporting easier management at week four / owners responding at baseline and follow-up | Establish baseline and disclose response rate and nonresponse bias. |
| Shared care | Households with a second caregiver viewing or recording / households inviting a caregiver | Establish baseline. |
| Offer redemption | Unique eligible recipients redeeming / unique eligible recipients | 10%, by offer and channel. |
| Incremental impact | Outcome difference between comparable test and control groups | Report uncertainty; gross redemptions are not incremental benefit. |
| Safety and trust | Reports, conflicting records, complaints, opt-outs, and escalation time | Agree thresholds with safety, legal, and operations before launch. |
| Clinic burden | Staff-reported introduction time and workload | Agree acceptable thresholds with clinics. |

## 14. Product Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Incorrect medication setup or conflicting caregiver records | Check against the prescription, use timestamps and conflict prevention, and flag stale status. |
| Owners assume observations are monitored | Show prominent not-monitored language, clinic contacts, and emergency guidance. |
| Adverse-event information enters the product | Define intake, monitoring, triage, training, and reporting processes before launch. |
| Promotion undermines clinical trust | Separate care content from promotion, disclose sponsorship, and prohibit incentives contingent on prescribing decisions. |
| Weak clinic adoption | Co-design with clinics, minimize staff steps, and measure burden. |
| Privacy or security failure | Minimize data, control access, define retention and incident response, and test security. |
| Misleading measurement | Use explicit denominators, self-report labels, controls where feasible, and transparent limitations. |
| Unsafe AI advice | Limit use cases, constrain outputs to reviewed content, require oversight, and prohibit autonomous treatment advice. |

FDA describes [veterinary adverse-event reporting for manufacturers](https://www.fda.gov/animal-veterinary/report-problem/veterinary-adverse-event-reporting-manufacturers). Product-specific obligations and workflows require regulatory and pharmacovigilance review.

## 15. Assumptions

- Owners want dedicated support beyond existing reminders.
- Clinics will introduce the program if setup is lightweight.
- Shared care is meaningful for a material segment.
- Owners will share summaries with veterinary teams.
- Education and eligible offers can coexist without reducing trust.
- Mobile web is sufficient for the initial audience.
- Partners can support promotion fulfillment and measurement.

Validate through interviews, prototype tests, clinic pilots, operational feasibility assessments, and observed ongoing behavior.

## 16. 12-Month Roadmap

| Period | Focus and deliverables | Decision gate |
|---|---|---|
| Months 1-3 | Research, prototypes, regulatory and safety assessment, and baseline measurement plan. | Confirm unmet need, feasible clinic workflow, and approved MVP scope. |
| Months 4-6 | Mobile web MVP, caregiver records, reminders, refill planning, observation log, clinic materials, consent, safety, and instrumentation. | Confirm usability, reliable records, operational safety readiness, and pilot participation. |
| Months 7-9 | Optimize onboarding, test approved offers and channels, add owner-shared summaries, and evaluate a controlled campaign or phased rollout. | Demonstrate owner value and acceptable clinic burden; assess early incremental results. |
| Months 10-12 | Expand if gates pass, publish findings, and assess integrations and expanded conditions. | Scale, revise, or stop based on outcomes, economics, safety, and readiness. |

An illustrative pilot recruits 15-25 clinics and enough owners to assess usability and directional engagement. Determine sample size for controlled evaluation separately.

## 17. AI and Personalization Opportunities

| Opportunity | Safeguard |
|---|---|
| Personalized education | Use reviewed content and transparent rules first; do not infer diagnoses. |
| Reminder preferences | Owner controls timing and frequency; do not alter prescribed timing. |
| Visit-summary assistance | Show source entries, label AI assistance, and require owner review before sharing. |
| Clinic enablement | Restrict answers to current approved sources; avoid clinical recommendations. |
| Marketing optimization | Use consented channels, aggregate measurement, frequency caps, and approved claims. |

Do not include AI diagnosis, autonomous triage, medication changes, prediction of individual treatment response, or automated clinical advice in the MVP. Future clinical uses require separate evidence and review.

## 18. Dependencies

Veterinary medical, regulatory, legal, compliance, pharmacovigilance, customer support, privacy, security, clinics, engineering, design, data and experimentation, marketing and commercial operations, pharmacy and technology partners, and an executive sponsor must agree ownership and launch readiness.

## 19. Executive Summary

Launch a gated, clinic-connected mobile-first experience that makes daily medication care easier and supports useful veterinary conversations. Start with US dog owners managing a daily oral chronic medication. Clinics introduce the program; owners use reminders, shared dose records, refill planning, observation tracking, and optional summaries; marketing uses consent-based campaigns and approved offers.

Fund discovery and a small pilot before national rollout. Scale only after demonstrating owner value, workable clinic adoption, appropriate safety controls, and credible incremental commercial impact. This proposal is distinct from the repository's existing vaccination-first plan and requires an explicit scope decision before merging plans.
